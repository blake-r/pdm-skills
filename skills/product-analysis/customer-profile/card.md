# Customer Profile

## Essence

A customer profile (Customer Profile) is a one-page description of a typical customer in four slices: who they are (About), in what context they use the product (Context), what is valuable to them (Value), and what problems they face (Problems). At the center is a conditional character with a name, so the team talks about a specific person rather than "users in general". This image is a convenient way to check decisions: will a feature help this customer, and with which of their problems?

## When to use and when not to

Use it during the initial design of a product or feature, when you need to agree on who it is for; when entering a new segment; when customers differ noticeably and one feature cannot suit everyone.

Inputs: survey results, interviews, product analytics and CRM data (customer and deal management systems) — customer characteristics, how and where they use the product, their goals and problems. If there are several types of customers, there are several profiles: one per segment.

Limits:

- **The average customer may not exist.** A profile averaged over different groups describes a person who does not exist; first split customers into segments, then describe each.
- **A profile ages.** Customers' context and problems change; a profile without a date and data source quickly becomes a myth.
- **A profile does not replace data.** The decision "to build or not" is checked on behavior.

## Scheme

At the center is the character: a name, one phrase about them, the segment. Around it are four quadrants.

| Quadrant | What is recorded |
|---|---|
| **About** | who they are: age, role, experience, income or business scale, spending structure, habits |
| **Context** | how, where and with what they use the product, when the need arises |
| **Value** | what matters to them, which goals they set, how they judge success |
| **Problems** | what gets in the way, where they lose money, time or nerves |

Next to it are the data source with a date and the conclusion for the product. The character's phrase, the segment and these two blocks are additions of this card to the original scheme.

## How to apply

1. **Choose the segment:** the profile describes one group of customers.
2. **Collect data:** surveys, interviews, analytics; each statement has a source.
3. **Fill the quadrants** with concrete facts: "sells up to 100 items", not "small business".
4. **Give the character a name** and one phrase that sets them apart from other segments.
5. **Compare the profiles** of different segments: where values and problems differ.
6. **Write the conclusion for the product:** which problem of which segment we solve and what we do not do.
7. **Update the profile** when new data arrives and put a date on it.

## Example

In 2025 the Tochka bank and the agency Data Insight surveyed 709 business leaders selling on Wildberries, Ozon and Yandex.Market, and described sellers with three portraits, by monthly turnover.

| | Up to 100K ₽ | 100K — 1M ₽ | Over 1M ₽ |
|---|---|---|---|
| **About** | 36–45 years old, 2–3 years of experience, up to 10 employees | under 35, over 3 years of experience | 46–55 years old, over 3 years of experience, 11–50 employees |
| **Context** | one marketplace, up to 100 products, accounting in Excel | 100–1,000 products, Excel, analytics and finance accounting services | several marketplaces, up to 3,000 products, own warehouse, analytics, finance, warehouse and accounting services |
| **Value** | margin (12%), turnover (11%), expansion to other platforms (10%) | turnover (25%), advertising efficiency (19%), new product lines (19%) | turnover (25%), advertising efficiency (23%), margin (22%) |
| **Problems** | returns (13%), traffic (10%), finding contractors (10%) | returns (21%), marketplace terms (14%), advertising (13%) | returns (22%), dishonest buyers (22%), advertising (19%) |

The figures in parentheses are the share of sellers who named the task or problem among the main ones. The report has no quadrants or characters: its sections "Portrait", "Business indicators", "Tools" and "Behavior" are laid out by quadrant for this card, and "Value" corresponds to the priority tasks of the business plan.

The profiles differ more than the "average seller" suggests: large ones have their own warehouse and warehouse and accounting systems, and they more often complain about dishonest buyers, while small ones want to enter new platforms. The common problem of all three is returns. Tochka did not name product decisions in the publication; the bank's seller segment lead concluded that "well-tuned operational processes and regular financial accounting" are becoming mandatory.

Source of the case: [Data Insight, "Sellers of universal marketplaces 2025"](https://datainsight.ru/DI_Tochka_marketplaces_sellers) (July 2025); the portraits and shares are in the report PDF linked on that page.

## Common mistakes

- **One profile for everyone.** The averaged customer hides that segments have different problems.
- **A profile from one's head.** The quadrants are filled with the team's assumptions without sources.
- **Demographics instead of context.** Age and gender are described in detail, but not how the customer works and where it hurts them.
- **A profile without a conclusion.** The portrait is drawn, but it is not said which problem the product solves.

## Source

The model is described in the [Product Architecture Framework](https://productframework.ru/activities/customer_research) by Sergey Tikhomirov (section Customer Research, licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0): a customer profile in four slices — general information, behavior in the context of using the product, values, problems — for quick or initial product or feature design. The name of the model has appeared on the framework's site [since 2018](https://web.archive.org/web/20181020213707/http://productframework.ru/customer_profile). A related technique is the proto-persona by Jeff Gothelf, [UX Magazine, 2012](https://uxmag.com/articles/using-proto-personas-for-executive-alignment).
