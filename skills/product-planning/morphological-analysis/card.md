# Morphological Analysis

## Essence

Morphological analysis is a way to systematically find variants of a product, feature or business model. The object is split into independent properties; for each property the possible values are listed, and the result is a table — a morphological box. A variant is a combination: one value from each row. The table shows the whole space of variants, including those the team would not have thought of, and helps to consciously choose several combinations instead of the first one that comes to mind.

## When to use and when not to

Use it when you need to invent or rebuild a product, plan, feature or sales channel; when the discussion revolves around two or three familiar options; when you need to show how the variants differ from one another.

Do not use it when the object cannot be split into independent properties, or when there are clearly too few variants. It does not replace testing: the table shows which combinations are possible, but not which ones customers need.

Limitations:

- **The number of combinations grows fast.** Five properties with four values each give 1,024 variants; without screening rules they cannot be reviewed.
- **Some combinations are incompatible.** Values of different properties may exclude each other; this is noted before enumeration.
- **The table is no better than the chosen properties.** A missing property will not appear in any variant.

## Scheme

| Property | Value 1 | Value 2 | Value 3 | Value 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Property 1** | 1.1 | 1.2 | 1.3 | 1.4 |
| **Property 2** | 2.1 | 2.2 | 2.3 | — |
| **Property 3** | 3.1 | 3.2 | 3.3 | 3.4 |

Variant A = 1.1 + 2.2 + 3.2; variant B = 1.4 + 2.2 + 3.3; variant C = 1.3 + 3.1 (property 2 not set). Dropped values and the reason for dropping them are recorded separately.

## How to apply

1. **Describe the object and the goal:** what is being rebuilt and for whom.
2. **Choose 4–6 properties** that do not depend on each other and matter to the customer or the business.
3. **List the values** for each property: familiar ones, those at competitors, and unusual ones.
4. **Mark incompatible pairs** and drop the values that fail the goal, the cost or the constraints; record the reason.
5. **Assemble combinations:** several meaningful variants, including unfamiliar ones.
6. **Compare and choose** by the criteria named in advance, and decide how to check the choice.

## Example

Monzo, a British digital bank, in April 2024 replaced its paid plans Plus and Premium with three new ones: Extra, Perks and Max. The bank does not mention morphological analysis; below, the plans from its blog are laid out by properties.

| Property | Values |
|---|---|
| **Price per month** | £3 · £7 · from £17 · £15 (previous Premium) |
| **Money management** | basic · extended: accounts at other banks, credit history; Extra adds virtual cards |
| **Savings** | standard rate · better rate 3.25% |
| **Perks** | none · weekly Greggs bakery items and an annual train discount · discounts at business lounges and cashback on international transfers |
| **Insurance** | none · travel, family — only on a joint trip (previous Premium) · phone, travel, roadside assistance, family — for £5 |
| **Card** | plastic · metal |

Combinations: Extra = £3 + extended money management + standard rate + no perks and no insurance; Perks = £7 + extended money management + 3.25% + Greggs and trains; Max = from £17 + everything in Perks + three insurance covers.

Dropped: the metal card — expensive; the bank preferred to put the money into more varied benefits; discounts at business lounges, special offers and cashback — according to the bank's research, only a small share of customers got the full benefit from them.

Result: according to the annual report for the year to 31 March 2025, more than 870 thousand personal customers pay for a plan (+64%); of these, more than 480 thousand moved to a paid plan for the first time, and 175 thousand from Plus and Premium. Subscription revenue grew by 50%. The bank notes that about 100 thousand upgrades came from the January savings promotion.

Sources of the case: [Monzo blog](https://monzo.com/blog/make-monzo-more-you) (Ben Lawrence, 23 April 2024) and [Monzo annual report for financial year 2025](https://monzo.com/annual-report/2025/Monzo%20Bank%20Group%20FY2025%20Annual%20Report.pdf).

## Common mistakes

- **Dependent properties.** Price and plan level in different rows give the same variants.
- **Only familiar values.** The table repeats the market and gives no new combinations.
- **Enumeration without screening.** Hundreds of variants are assessed by hand instead of first removing the incompatible ones.
- **Choice without criteria.** The combination that the lead liked wins.
