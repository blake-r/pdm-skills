# Six Thinking Hats

## Essence

The Six Thinking Hats is a way to run a discussion or make a decision by looking at a question in turn from one mode of thinking. Each mode is designated by the colour of a hat: facts, feelings, risks, benefits, new options and process management. At every moment the whole group thinks in one mode, so the argument between optimists and critics becomes two separate steps.

## When to use and when not to

Use it when you need to choose between several options and the discussion goes in circles; when one voice (most often a critic or an enthusiast) drowns out the others; when a decision is made under pressure and emotions mix with facts. It also suits one person: the hats help lay out thoughts before a difficult decision.

Do not use it when the decision is obvious or cheap to reverse, when the data itself is lacking (it is collected first), and when the question is purely computational.

Inputs: the question in one sentence, known facts with sources, the decision options if they already exist, and the participants on whom the decision depends.

Limitations:

- **The method orders thinking but does not add knowledge.** If there are no facts, the white hat will show this, but will not fill the gap.
- **The result depends on the facilitator.** Without strict adherence to the sequence the discussion slides back into an ordinary argument.
- **The effect of the method in business is poorly measured.**

## Scheme

| Hat | Mode of thinking | Question |
|---|---|---|
| **White** | facts and data | what do we know and what is missing? |
| **Red** | feelings and intuition | what do we feel, without explanations? |
| **Black** | risks and criticism | what could go wrong and why? |
| **Yellow** | benefits and optimism | what will it bring and why is it realistic? |
| **Green** | new ideas | what other options and improvements are there? |
| **Blue** | process management | in what order do we think, and what did we decide? |

The blue hat opens and closes the discussion: it sets the question and the order, and at the end records the decision. The order of the other hats is chosen to suit the task.

## How to apply

1. **Formulate the question** in one sentence (blue hat) and appoint a facilitator. About two minutes are usually spent on each hat, and longer on the white hat at the start.
2. **Choose the order.** For a choice between options de Bono proposes: blue, white, (green), yellow, black, red, blue.
3. **Think in one mode.** All participants "put on" one hat at the same time; the facilitator postpones thoughts of another mode until its turn.
4. **Record** the conclusions under each hat, 3–5 points each. Red takes about 30 seconds: a reaction without justification.
5. **Go through the options under the black and yellow hats** one by one, so that each has both risks and benefits.
6. **Close with the blue hat:** the decision, the owner, the deadline and a sign that the decision has worked.

## Example

An indie developer (evgeny1709, Habr) launched a Chrome extension for a European audience; promotion was built on Google Ads search advertising. At the final stage of the launch Google blocked the ad account. The options were: an appeal, an agency account, grey schemes with rented accounts, and reworking the product for Yandex. The author went through the hats in his own order. This case is not from e-commerce or fintech: no public write-ups of that kind were found.

| Hat | The author's conclusions (abridged) |
|---|---|
| **White** | the product and offers are tailored to Google search traffic; Google has a dominant share of search in Europe; appeals can drag on for weeks with no guarantees; switching to another channel means months of work |
| **Red** | anger at the block without explanations; fear of losing everything in grey schemes and burying the idea by moving to Yandex; fatigue from correspondence with support |
| **Black** | an appeal means weeks without revenue; an agency account carries the risk of a hard block; grey schemes mean a chain ban and blocked payments; Yandex means months of work for a channel weak for the audience |
| **Yellow** | an unban keeps the legal channel; an agency account quickly returns the ability to test; grey schemes are a fast start, but the price is too high; another channel reduces dependence on a single platform |
| **Green** | an appeal in parallel with an agency account; content marketing, affiliate programmes and extension catalogues; several ad networks |
| **Blue** | the decision is below |

Decision: give up grey schemes; file an appeal and in parallel buy an account from an agency, working without rule violations; look at other channels without reworking the product. The metrics for checking are the cost of a click and an install, the conversion into the target action in the extension, and the stability of the account in the first weeks. The result is not published in the article.

Source of the case: ["The six thinking hats method: how to make a decision", Habr](https://habr.com/ru/articles/977024/) (16 December 2025).

## Common mistakes

- **Hats by person.** Each participant is given their own hat permanently, and the "black" one criticises the whole discussion. The whole group puts on the hat at once.
- **Mixing modes.** Under the white hat they argue about risks, under the yellow they look for flaws.
- **Skipping the red hat.** Feelings are not voiced and later pass as arguments.
- **No blue hat at the end.** The discussion ends with lists, and there is no decision, owner or deadline.
