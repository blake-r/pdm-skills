# Storytelling

## Essence

Storytelling for a product is a way to build a story about an idea so that the listener makes a decision, not just learns facts. The scheme here is Nancy Duarte's Presentation Sparkline (a line drawn along the story): the story alternates "what is" (the audience's world) and "what could be" (the world with the product). The gap grows and leads to a call to action.

## When to use and when not to

Use it when you need to persuade: a pitch to investors or management, a launch, the defence of an initiative. Inputs: the audience, the desired decision, verifiable facts.

Card author's conclusion: do not use it for reference material and specifications, where data is needed rather than persuasion.

Limitations:

- **Card author's conclusion: "what is" must be checkable.** A status quo made of the speaker's own figures or mood is easy to dismiss and weakens the gap.
- **This is an observation, not a law.** Duarte laid over each other the speech of Martin Luther King and the launch of the iPhone in 2007; the number of transitions is not specified.
- **The audience resists.** The story moves in zigzags, like a sailing ship against the wind.

## Scheme

```
BEGINNING  what is ──► what could be
MIDDLE     what is ◄─► what could be
END        call to action ──► "new bliss"
```

The line is drawn over time: peaks are "what could be", troughs are "what is". In the template the line is drawn by a chart: one point per beat of the story, 0 — "is", 1 — "could be".

## How to apply

1. **Learn about the audience** with Duarte's three questions: what problem keeps them up at night; what they have already tried without success; what is at stake if nothing changes.
2. **Name the decision** you expect. It becomes the call to action.
3. **Beginning.** Show "what is" with facts and set "what could be" against it. Strengthen the gap.
4. **Middle.** Compile pairs "today — with the product" and alternate them.
5. **End.** The call to action goes at the very end. Then describe the world after the solution ("new bliss"), not a list of features.
6. **Check the line.** If there is no alternation, no gap, or the call is not at the end — rewrite.

## Example

Apple Pay (fintech). Presentation on 9 September 2014, Tim Cook and Eddy Cue. Pairs compiled by the card author.

| Step | What is | What could be |
|---|---|---|
| Beginning | In the US, $12 billion and more than 200 million transactions a day; the magnetic stripe is five decades old | Cook's vision: replace the wallet, starting with payments |
| Middle 1 | The mobile wallet, according to the New York Times (Jenna Wortham), was long considered a matter of the near future, but stayed elusive | iPhone 6 with NFC (Near Field Communication, contactless communication) and Touch ID (fingerprint sensor) |
| Middle 2 | A card is easy to lose, the cashier sees the name and number | Secure Element (chip for payment data); one-time number; Apple does not know what was bought |
| Middle 3 | Online in the US: more than $1 billion a day through long forms | Payment with one touch |
| Middle 4 | — | More than 220,000 contactless payment points; banks — more than 80% of US credit card volume |
| Call | — | Launch in the US next month, free iOS 8 update (the iPhone system) |
| New bliss | — | Cook: "Apple Pay will forever change the way all of us buy things" |

Line (order of the talk; 0/1 assigned by the card's author): vision 1, figures 0, stripe 0, NYT 0, Apple Pay 1, security 1, stores 1, online 0, tap 1, call 1.

Decision: launch in the US on 20 October 2014 with iOS 8.1 (Apple release of 16 October), more than 500 new banks.

What the case lacks: Duarte's line; a call to action and "new bliss" in her sense (the rows are the closest in meaning remarks); the order in the table was reassembled: the pain runs as one solid block before the solution, and the alternation is only "payments — online"; data on the argument that influenced the banks; the launch decision was made before the presentation. The figures $12 billion and $1 billion appear only in the keynote transcript, not in Apple's release; they are Apple's own claims, not independently checked.

Sources: [transcript](https://singjupost.com/apple-iphone-6-keynote-september-2014-launch-event-full-transcript/); [release of 9 September 2014](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2014/09/09Apple-Announces-Apple-Pay/); [release of 16 October 2014](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2014/10/16Apple-Pay-Set-to-Transform-Mobile-Payments-Starting-October-20/).

## Common mistakes

- **A story only about the product.** Without "what is" there is no gap and no reason to act.
- **Card author's conclusion: a neutral "what is".** It has no cost for the audience.
- **One transition instead of alternation.** First the whole problem, then the whole solution.
- **The call is not at the end.** It is missing or in the middle.
- **Card author's conclusion: the end is a list of features.** There is no picture of the world.
- **Unverifiable pain.** Figures without a source; check the speaker's words.
- **Card author's conclusion: structure instead of proof.** Smooth does not mean it works.
