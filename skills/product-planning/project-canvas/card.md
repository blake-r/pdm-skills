# Project Canvas

## Essence

The project canvas is a description of a project on one sheet: why it exists, for whom, who takes part, what the goals, work, deliverables, risks, milestones, constraints and boundaries are. A regular project description document is long, nobody reads it, and the logic of the project stays invisible. The canvas brings it onto one sheet, which the team fills in together at the start and keeps in front of them until the end.

## When to use and when not to

Use it at the start of a project: at a briefing with the client as a list of questions, at a kick-off meeting so that the whole team has the same understanding of the goal and boundaries; during the project as a reference to return to when disputes arise about scope.

Do not use it in place of a plan and a budget: the canvas contains no task deadlines, resources or costs. For a small task for a single team it is redundant.

Limitations:

- **The canvas is a snapshot.** When deadlines or scope change, it must be updated, otherwise it misleads.
- **Not everything fits.** The author deliberately left assumptions and critical success factors outside the frame.
- **It works only with the team's participation.** A canvas filled in by one manager does not create shared understanding.

## Scheme

| Block | What is recorded |
|---|---|
| **Project** (top corner) | the project name |
| **Motivation** (top) | why the project exists and what prompted it to start |
| **Participants** | core team, stakeholders, third-party participants; dependencies |
| **Goals** | project goals and success metrics |
| **Users** (centre) | target groups |
| **User benefits** | what users will get when the project is complete |
| **Activities** | key work and approaches |
| **Deliverables** | what will be handed over and seen by others |
| **Risks** | events that may get in the way, and how to reduce them |
| **Milestones** | key dates and events |
| **Constraints** | external conditions that must not be violated: regulation, platforms, standards |
| **Scope** | what is included in the project and what is not |
| **End** (bottom) | the date or decision with which the project ends |

## How to apply

1. **Gather the team and stakeholders** for a kick-off meeting; print the canvas in large format.
2. **Start with the motivation and users**, then the goals with metrics and the benefits.
3. **Fill in participants, activities and deliverables:** who does what and what is handed over.
4. **Write the risks with measures, milestones, constraints and scope,** including what is not part of the project.
5. **Name the end:** the launch date or the decision with which the project is closed.
6. **Keep the canvas in sight** and update it when deadlines or scope change.

## Example

Open Banking is a project under which the nine largest UK banks (CMA9) opened access to account data and payments through standard interfaces. The regulator did not use a canvas; below, its published decisions are laid out by block. Details not found in the sources are marked.

| Block | Open Banking |
|---|---|
| **Project** | Open Banking: access to accounts through standards |
| **Motivation** | retail banking market investigation, final report of 9 August 2016: remedies to make banks compete harder for customers, including Open Banking |
| **Participants** | CMA9 (fund the project, shares by market share); Open Banking Implementation Entity led by an independent trustee; oversight — the competition regulator CMA, working with the FCA and HM Treasury |
| **Goals** | implement Open Banking by early 2018, making it easier to compare products and switch to better deals while protecting privacy and security; no success metrics found in the sources, later reporting counts active users |
| **Users and benefits** | retail customers and small businesses: compare products, switch to better deals, share data with third parties securely |
| **Deliverables** | open API standards and frameworks, including security arrangements |
| **Milestones** | early 2018 — deadline for the first interfaces; May 2020 — revised roadmap |
| **Risks** | banks fall behind: in April 2019 the regulator issued directions to five banks for delays, and four met the deadline |
| **Constraints** | the CMA order of 2017 (Retail Banking Market Investigation Order); security and privacy protection of customers |
| **Scope** | the Roadmap required by the Order for the nine banks; expansion beyond the Order is outside the project |
| **End** | the CMA decision that the roadmap has been completed |

Decision: in May 2020 the regulator approved a revised roadmap with extended timelines because of the Covid-19 crisis, and incorporated it into the Order. At completion the CMA said it would work with the FCA, the Payment Systems Regulator and HM Treasury on future arrangements beyond the Order.

Result: on 12 January 2023, six of the largest banks met all requirements, and services based on Open Banking had more than 6 million active users. On 9 September 2024, all nine banks completed the project; in July 2024 there were 11.32 million users.

Sources of the case: [CMA press release of 9 August 2016](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/cma-paves-the-way-for-open-banking-revolution), [implementation entity plans](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5800ddf3e5274a67eb000000/Implementation_entity_plans_and_proposals.pdf), [CMA press release](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/millions-of-customers-benefit-as-open-banking-reaches-milestone) (12 January 2023), Open Banking news on the [directions to banks](https://www.openbanking.org.uk/news/cma-issues-directions-to-5-banks/) (1 April 2019), the [roadmap](https://www.openbanking.org.uk/news/cma-publishes-approved-roadmap-for-the-final-stages-of-open-banking-implementation/) (15 May 2020) and the [completion](https://www.openbanking.org.uk/news/cma-confirms-full-completion-of-open-banking-roadmap-unlocking-a-new-era-of-financial-innovation/) (9 September 2024).

## Common mistakes

- **Goals without metrics.** It is impossible to tell whether the goal has been reached.
- **An empty scope block.** It is not written what is outside the project, and the scope grows.
- **Risks without measures.** A list of dangers that nothing is done about.
- **The canvas on the wall and forgotten.** The deadlines and scope have changed, but the sheet has not.
