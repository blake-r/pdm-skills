# Value Proposition Canvas

## Essence

The Value Proposition Canvas is a template that matches what a product offers with what a specific segment of customers needs. On the right, in the circle, is the customer side: their jobs, pains and desired gains. On the left, in the square, is the value map: products and services, what they relieve pains with, and what they create gains with. Comparing the two sides shows whether the offer covers the pains and gains that matter to the customer.

## When to use and when not to

Use it when designing a new product or a major feature and you need to tie each decision to a pain or gain found in research; when an offer does not sell and you need to understand what it lacks.

Do not use it without data about customers: a canvas built on guesses will cement them.

Inputs: the segment, results of interviews, surveys and analytics, the current offer and the alternatives customers use.

Limitations:

- **One canvas — one segment.** Different segments have different jobs and pains.
- **Fit on paper is not demand.** The canvas is a hypothesis; the importance to the customer is checked by sales or behaviour.

## Scheme

| Part | Block | What is recorded |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer side (circle; on the slide — "Customer segment")** | Jobs (Customer Jobs) | what the customer is trying to do: functional, social, emotional jobs |
| | Pains | what gets in the way, irritates, what risks they face before, during and after the job |
| | Gains | what they want to achieve, what would delight them |
| **Value map (square; on the slide — "Value proposition")** | Products & Services | what we offer |
| | Pain Relievers | how the offer relieves specific pains |
| | Gain Creators | how it delivers specific gains |

Fit exists when the pain relievers and gain creators answer the most important pains and gains of the segment.

## How to apply

1. **Choose the segment** and collect data about it.
2. **Fill in the customer side:** jobs, pains, gains — in the customers' wording, with a source. Rank the jobs by importance, the pains from extreme to moderate, and the gains from essential to desirable; the basis is frequency in interviews, share in a survey, money or time lost.
3. **Describe the products and services** you offer or plan to offer.
4. **For each important pain** write what relieves it; for each important gain write what creates it.
5. **Check the fit:** which important pains and gains are not covered; which features answer nothing.
6. **Decide** what to do, what to remove, and how to check that customers care about it.

## Example

Monzo (a British digital bank) built Flex — buying now and paying later in instalments. The team studied how customers use credit cards and "buy now, pay later" services. The team did not use the canvas: it condensed its findings into four principles: certainty, control, visibility, convenience. The article does not rank the importance of pains and gains. Below, its documented findings and decisions are laid out by the canvas blocks.

| Block | Content from the Monzo article |
|---|---|
| **Jobs** | pay for a purchase later: a wedding dress, a work laptop, an unexpected pet bill; postpone payment until the refund for an online clothing order arrives |
| **Pains** | on a credit card it is hard to track individual purchases, and you end up paying the total; it is hard to tell the minimum payment from the real debt; payments in different apps are hard to track; an application has to be submitted again for each purchase |
| **Gains** | know in advance what will be approved and know your limit; feel in control of your finances; see how repayment is progressing |
| **Products & Services** | Flex in the Monzo app: pay later online, in store, and for purchases already made on the current account (within two weeks) |
| **Pain Relievers** | fixed repayment over 3, 6 or 12 payments with a clear end date and a monthly amount; one limit for all purchases without repeat applications; everything next to the current account |
| **Gain Creators** | the customer chooses the limit themselves after a credit check; a repayment progress bar for each purchase |

Decision: launch Flex with these features and an ongoing research programme.

Result a year later: hundreds of thousands of customers use the product; according to the company, its Net Promoter Score (NPS) leads the market (the figures themselves are not published).

Source of the case: [Kate Kerr, Antonio Angrisani, "How insights shaped building a 0-1 product: Monzo Flex", Monzo blog](https://monzo.com/blog/2022/11/16/how-insights-shaped-building-a-0-1-product-monzo-flex) (18 November 2022).

## Common mistakes

- **Starting with the product.** The square is filled first, and the circle is fitted to ready-made features.
- **Pains from one's head.** The customer side is filled with the team's assumptions, without sources.
- **Everything in a row.** All pains are listed without priority, and it is not clear which matter more.
- **Features without a pair.** The square contains features that answer no pain or gain, and they are not removed.
