# Team Canvas

## Essence

Team Canvas is a sheet of nine blocks that a team fills in together at one meeting to agree on goals, values and working rules. Authors: Alex Ivanov and Mitya (Dmitry) Voloshchuk, 2015, licensed under CC BY-SA 4.0.

The authors rely on a model of group development stages: teams often get stuck at the conflict stage or never find time to resolve conflicts. The canvas is a reason to ask hard questions in time.

The blocks answer four questions: what kind of team this is (people, roles, goals), why it works (purpose, values), who is in it (strengths, weaknesses, needs), and how it will work (rules).

Versions: full — 90–120 minutes; basic — 30–45 minutes, five blocks: goals, roles and skills, purpose, values, rules. Participants: 2–8. On another page of the site the authors give other timings: about two hours and 25–30 minutes.

## When to use and when not to

Use at the launch of a team or project, when a new participant joins, or when goals have blurred or tension is building up. The authors say the full version can gently resolve conflicts, and recommend repeating it every two to three months.

Do not use it to analyze an acute conflict: according to SI Labs, the canvas needs at least a minimal willingness to talk, and such a conflict is handled with a mediator.

Limitations:

- **There is no research on effectiveness.** Even SI Labs consultants admit this.
- **Weaknesses require trust.** According to SI Labs, if mistakes are punished, the block will stay empty.
- **The canvas gives a frame, not answers.** The authors: all answers are acceptable; the facilitator does not hint at the "right" ones.

## How to apply

1. **Prepare** a board or an A0–A1 sheet, stickers of different colors for each person, and a timer. The facilitator is the team leader or an invited person.
2. **Announce the conversation rules:** do not interrupt, do not criticize others' answers, speak one at a time.
3. **Go through the blocks with a timer.** Each person writes answers on stickers and talks about them.

| Block | Min | Authors' question |
|---|---|---|
| 1 People and roles* | 5 | what are we called, what are our roles? |
| 2 Common goals* | 10 | what do we want to achieve — feasible, measurable, by when? |
| 3 Personal goals | 5 | what does each person want for themselves in this project? |
| 4 Purpose* | 10 | why do we do this at all? |
| 5 Values* | 10 | what do we stand for? |
| 6 Strengths | 15 | which skills will help reach the goals? |
| 7 Weaknesses | 15 | what weaknesses does each person and the team have? |
| 8 Needs | 10 | what does each person need to succeed? |
| 9 Rules* | 10 | what rules do we introduce, how do we communicate and decide? |

\* agreed by the whole team; the rest each person may fill in alone.

4. **In the weaknesses block** each person talks about themselves, not about others.
5. **Park long disputes** and schedule a separate meeting for them.
6. **Finish** in 5 minutes: each person names the main takeaway. Keep the canvas where everyone can see it, and schedule the next session.

Interpretation: the rules must cover the weaknesses and needs named on the canvas.

## Example

Retail Rocket makes product recommendations for online stores. Some developers work in Moscow, some remotely, and there are newcomers.

The full version for developers and analysts took about two and a half hours plus a break.

| Block | What came out |
|---|---|
| Values and purpose | the hardest to agree on: vague wording such as "release the best product" — best by which criteria? A common wording was agreed after discussion, not published |
| Personal goals, needs | everyone perceives the environment, office comfort and the pace of their own development differently |

Decisions: the canvas was hung as an A1 poster; team leads included personal goals and expectations in employee development plans; open questions are closed with new rules; they agreed to repeat the session every six months.

What the case lacks: the filled-in canvas (not a single agreed wording) and figures: the result is described as "exclusively positive" reviews. The company supplies technology to online stores rather than selling anything itself.

Case source: N. Kazmina, [«How team building works at Retail Rocket and why we chose Team Canvas»](https://retailrocket.ru/blog/kak-prohodit-timbilding-v-retail-rocket-i-pochemu-my-vybrali-team-canvas), 22.06.2021.

## Common mistakes

The mistakes are compiled from SI Labs and the method's author:

- **Starting with the rules.** Without purpose and values, rules turn into bureaucracy.
- **Values as decoration.** "Respect, trust, openness" without a description of behavior changes nothing.
- **Fill in and forget.** The author called one-off use the main difficulty of the method.
- **A group that is too large.** More than ten people means not everyone can be heard; split into subgroups.
- **Rules without consequences.** Agree what happens if a rule is broken.
- **Discussing others' weaknesses.** The session turns into a list of grievances.

## See also

- Enhanced by Retrospective Meeting: Repeat the canvas every two to three months and check the agreed rules.
- Followed by Objectives and Key Results (OKR): turn the common goals agreed on the canvas into OKRs.
- Enhanced by Project Canvas: Both put goals, participants and roles on one sheet; this one describes a team.
- Enhanced by Definition of Done: the rules agreed on the canvas can include when work counts as finished.
- Followed by Stand-up Meeting: Agree on roles and working rules on the canvas, then hold daily stand-ups.
