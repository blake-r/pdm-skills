# Stakeholders Matrix

## Essence

The Stakeholders Matrix is a 2×2 table on which people and groups connected with a product are placed along two axes. Vertically — how much the person can influence the product or the decision: approve, block, give or withdraw resources. Horizontally — how much the product will affect that person: their work, money, risks. In the classic version, the second axis is the person's interest in the product.

Each quadrant corresponds to its own way of working:

- high influence on the product and strong impact — **manage closely**: involve them in decisions;
- high influence, weak impact — **keep satisfied**: address their concerns without overloading them with details;
- weak influence, strong impact — **keep informed**: report regularly and listen;
- weak influence and weak impact — **monitor**.

## When to use and when not to

Use it at the start of a product, project or major change, when the decision depends on many parties: a regulator, partners, other teams, management. When a launch can be stopped by someone nobody thought about. When you need to decide where to spend the team's time.

Do not use it if there are two or three parties and all are known: the matrix will add nothing to the conversation. It is not suitable as a one-off document: the influence and interest of parties change over the course of a project.

Limitations:

- **A position in the matrix is the team's estimate.** Two people will place the same parties differently; disputed positions are discussed aloud.
- **A group is not uniform.** Inside "banks" or "management" there are supporters and opponents; they are placed separately.
- **The matrix does not say whether a party is for or against.** This is marked separately, for example by colour.

## Scheme

```
 influence of the party on the product
    ▲
 high│  keep satisfied    │  manage closely
    │  address concerns  │  involve in decisions
    ├───────────────────┼──────────────────────
    │  monitor           │  keep informed
 low│  track             │  report and listen
    └───────────────────┴──────────────────────▶
      low       impact of the product on the party     high
```

## How to apply

1. **Describe the subject:** the product, project or decision the matrix is built for, and the deadline.
2. **List the parties:** customers, users, management, adjacent teams, partners, suppliers, regulators. Record supporters and opponents within one group separately.
3. **Assess both axes** for each party: high or low, with a short justification.
4. **Place the parties in quadrants** and mark who is for and who is against.
5. **For each quadrant, write the way of working:** who, how often, in what format.
6. **Revisit the matrix** at each major stage.

## Example

M-Pesa is a mobile phone money transfer service launched by Safaricom in Kenya. In August 2006, Safaricom asked the Central Bank of Kenya (CBK) to allow a nationwide launch. Former CBK governor Njuguna Ndung'u described how he made the decision, and the parties — "the main players in this game". We sorted them into quadrants:

| Quadrant | Who | What was done with them |
|---|---|---|
| Manage closely | Safaricom and the CBA bank, where clients' money is held | conditions: money in a trust account at the bank, monthly reports to the CBK on transactions, balances and inactive accounts |
| Keep satisfied | international banks, which warned of a banking crisis; the deputy governor, who expected mass bank failures; the Ministry of Finance | a demonstration of how the service works and candid questions from the CBK and ministries |
| Keep informed | Kenyan banks and microfinance institutions, afraid of losing small depositors | an explanation: M-Pesa is a tool they should integrate with |
| Monitor | the Ministry of Communications, satisfied with the participation of telecom operators | — |

Decision: after the demonstration no serious questions remained, and the CBK approved the launch. Result: in March 2007, the launch month, there were 21,714 transactions; in April, 69,740; in December, 1.27 million.

What the case does not have: the CBK did not draw a matrix. The governor described the parties, their strength and concerns in words; the quadrants are our markup. This is a regulator's decision about someone else's product, not a decision of the product team.

Source of the case: N. S. Ndung'u, [«A Digital Financial Services Revolution in Kenya: The M-Pesa Case Study»](https://aercafrica.org/old-website/wp-content/uploads/2021/03/AERC-MPesa-Case-Study.pdf), African Economic Research Consortium.

## Common mistakes

- **Forgetting parties without a formal role.** A regulator, support service or lawyers appear at a moment when it is already too late.
- **Putting everyone in "manage closely".** The team drowns in meetings, while the important parties get less attention.
- **Confusing influence with position.** Sometimes an expert, not a manager, has the strongest influence on the launch.
- **Building it once.** A party that was neutral becomes an opponent, and the matrix does not show this.
- **Not recording the way of working.** The quadrants are set, but who communicates with the party and how is not decided.
