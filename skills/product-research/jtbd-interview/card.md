# JTBD Interview

## Essence

JTBD (Jobs-to-be-Done, "the job for which the customer hires a product") is a view in which a person buys a product to make progress in a specific situation. A JTBD interview (Switch Interview) is a conversation about a recent purchase or a change of decision. The researcher reconstructs the chronology from the first thought to the start of use and looks for four forces of choice. PAF (Product Architecture Framework, productframework.ru) describes the method as an interview about the context of the decision, motivation and outcome, taking competitors into account.

## When to use and when not to

Use it to understand why people chose, switched or rejected a product. Inputs: recent buyers, consent to record, two researchers.

Do not use it:

- **For measuring shares.** PAF: the method is not suitable for quantitative assessment of behaviour. Forces and the order of events give hypotheses, not percentages.
- **For complex organisations.** Chris Spiek: owners of small businesses describe a purchase well, but in large companies committees make decisions, and the course of the decision is hard to reconstruct.
- **If there are no buyers yet.**

Cost (PAF): 30 minutes to 1.5 hours per interview, a prepared interviewer.

## Scheme

```
First thought → Passive search → Event 1 → Active search → Event 2 → Decision → Use
```

"Use" was added by the card's author: on the site, the last stage is called "Decision, purchase and consumption". Below the line are four forces: **push** (what does not suit now), **pull** (what the new one promises), **anxiety** (what is feared in the new), **habit** (what holds on to the old). A switch happens if push plus pull is stronger than anxiety plus habit (Moesta and Spiek).

## How to apply

1. **Find recent buyers or those who changed their decision.** The sources do not set a strict deadline.
2. **Start from the "Decision" point.** Place the other events as the story unfolds, going back in time.
3. **Look for the first thought deeper than the one named.** People name the moment later than the real one. Ask what happened before.
4. **Find out what was tried and considered,** including workarounds.
5. **Mark the forces at each event** and the person's verbatim words. Work in pairs: one leads the conversation, the other records (PAF advice).
6. **Conduct 10–12 interviews** per segment (PAF advises at least 12 for interviews in general; Spiek says about ten) and compare the chronologies. Repeated events and forces give the formulation of the job.
7. **Make a decision:** strengthen the pull, reduce anxiety or habit, or change the offer. Check it on customer behaviour (author's conclusion).

## Example

Autobooks is a fintech service: small businesses issue invoices and accept cards through internet banking. At the BoS (Business of Software) USA 2023 conference, Spiek (with Justin Dickow) said that at the start of the work he ran about ten back-to-back interviews. The source gives both 45 and 90 days for the starting period.

The chronology of Harry, an attorney in his 50s or 60s whose clients paid by cheque:

| Marker | What the source says |
|---|---|
| First thought | A client asked if they could pay by card; Harry declined, but the idea stayed (the source does not label it "first thought") |
| Passive search | A friend who handles smaller cases used Square; it did not seem a fit; Harry also dismissed PayPal ("I'm an attorney, I'm a professional") |
| Event 1 | Two or three questions about the card over two weeks |
| Decision | Connected Autobooks, which his bank offered ("if my bank can help me out, I'm going to put it in") |

The source does not name event 2 or the dates between the markers. Forces (reconstruction by the card's author): push — repeated client requests; pull — the service of his own bank; habit — cheques. Anxiety is not in the source.

Conclusions (Spiek): owners think about accounting twice a year, but about getting money every day; some businesses do not issue invoices. Decisions: accounting was separated and made a paid extension; calls to action in internet banking became "you need to get paid" and "you need to issue an invoice"; a payment link was added.

Not in the source: event 2 and dates, anxiety, analysis of the forces, numerical results of the decisions. The account is given by the author of the method.

Source of the case: [Spiek, Dickow, «Autobooks: JTBD, Product & Engineering»](https://businessofsoftware.org/talks/autobooks-jtbd-product-and-engineering/), BoS Conference USA, 2023.

## Common mistakes

- **Believing the named date of the first thought.** The real one is earlier: buyers named April visits to demonstration homes, while for 90% (according to Moesta's claim on his site) the first thought came between Thanksgiving and New Year.
- **Asking about desired features.** Before the search, a person does not know what they want: they clarify it while choosing. Ask about events.
- **Talking only to those who bought.** Anxiety and habit are visible in those who left and those who did not decide (conclusion of the card's author).
- **Taking one story as the rule.** One interview is a hypothesis; Spiek says there were plenty of people like Harry, which only repeated interviews can show.
- **Looking for a single pull.** According to Moesta and Spiek, the outcome is often decided by anxiety and habit.
- **Expecting numbers.** Ten interviews do not give shares.
- **Taking committees without a plan.** The history of the decision cannot be reconstructed in one conversation.
