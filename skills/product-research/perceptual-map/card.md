# Perceptual Map

## Essence

A Perceptual Map is a diagram on which brands or products are placed the way buyers see them. The axes are dimensions by which buyers tell offers apart: price and quality, convenience and choice, reliability and novelty. Points that stand close together are seen by buyers as similar, distant ones as different. The circle size often shows market share.

The map answers two questions: on which dimensions buyers compare offers at all, and where your product stands on those dimensions relative to others. An empty place on the map is a candidate for a free position, if there are buyers there.

## When to use and when not to

Use it when you need to decide how the product should differ from others in the buyer's eyes: before a launch, before a price or advertising message change, or when sales fall without a visible cause. When the team argues whether they are "expensive" or "convenient", the map replaces the argument with data.

Do not use it if buyers do not know the brands being compared: the ratings will be invented. It is not suitable for a market with one or two players: there is nothing to compare with.

Limitations:

- **The map shows perception, not fact.** If buyers consider the product expensive when it is cheap, this is a task for communication, not for price.
- **Either you or the statistics choose the axes.** When rating by given attributes, the axes are clear, but an important attribute may be missed. When rating pairwise similarity, the calculation finds the axes, and naming them is a guess.
- **An empty place does not mean demand.** A position may be empty because nobody needs it.

## Scheme

```
        quality ▲
           high  │   ● A               ◯ empty
                 │             ●● B
   ──────────────┼──────────────────────▶ price
      cheap      │     ● C             expensive
           low   │            ● ours
                 │
   ● — brand, circle size — market share
```

## How to apply

1. **Choose the brands:** those between which the buyer really chooses, from four to eight.
2. **Find the dimensions.** Ask buyers in interviews or in a focus group what distinguishes the brands, and condense the answers into five to ten attributes.
3. **Collect ratings.** Ask buyers to rate each brand on each attribute, or to rate the similarity of pairs of brands. Dozens of answers per brand are needed, preferably more than a hundred.
4. **Build maps** for pairs of the most important dimensions, or obtain the axes by calculation. Add circle size if shares are known.
5. **Read the map:** who is next to you, where it is empty, where the leaders stand.
6. **Decide** where to move and with what lever: price, features or message. Repeat the survey after changes.

## Example

From January to early February 2016, Natasha Adellia and Adhi Prasetio surveyed 385 Indonesian buyers online about five marketplaces and, from the answers, built a perceptual map with multidimensional scaling (MDS). The axes were found by calculation; the authors did not name them.

| Marketplace | Dimension 1 | Dimension 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Tokopedia | 1.40 | −0.51 |
| Bukalapak | 1.62 | 0.28 |
| Elevenia | −0.34 | 0.82 |
| Qoo10 | −1.65 | 0.13 |
| Rakuten | −1.03 | −0.72 |

Only Tokopedia and Bukalapak stand on the right; the other three are on the left. The respondents rated the sites on five attributes (usability, site design, information quality, trust, empathy); the abstract names Tokopedia and Bukalapak as perceived most ideal, followed by Elevenia, Qoo10 and Rakuten.

The decision the authors propose: marketplaces should improve their sites so that they match buyer perception.

What was happening in the market: in February 2016, while the survey was running, Rakuten announced the closure of its Indonesian marketplace from 1 March.

What the case does not have: this is an independent study. No marketplace made decisions based on this map, and Rakuten's exit is not linked to it — the company announced it at the same time as the survey.

Source of the case: N. Adellia, A. Prasetio, [«Customer perception mapping analysis of Indonesian e-commerce marketplace sites…»](https://doi.org/10.1109/CITSM.2016.7577580), International Conference on Cyber and IT Service Management, 2016; on Rakuten's closure — [The Jakarta Post, 15 February 2016](https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2016/02/15/rakuten-closes-down-indonesia.html).

## Common mistakes

- **The team chooses the axes, not the buyers.** The map shows differences that matter to marketers, not to those who choose.
- **Drawing from the team's opinion.** Without a survey, the map repeats what the team already thinks about itself.
- **Naming axes by guesswork and believing the name.** An axis from calculation is a hypothesis; it is checked with separate questions.
- **Treating any empty place as an opportunity.** First check whether there are buyers there.
- **Building it once.** Perception changes after each advertising campaign and the arrival of a new player.
