# PESTEL Analysis

## Essence

PESTEL is a way to describe the external environment of a company or product through six groups of factors: political, economic, social, technological, environmental and legal. For each group, verifiable facts are collected so that threats and new opportunities are noticed in time.

## When to use and when not to

Use it when entering a new country or industry, revising strategy, launching a product in a regulated area (payments, lending), or when external conditions change sharply. You need: the subject of the analysis, the country, the horizon, and open sources.

Do not use it to choose a feature or to assess a competitor.

Limitations:

- **There is no priority.** The output is a set of facts without an assessment of how strongly they affect the business. Ranking is a separate step.
- **It is a snapshot at a date:** the environment changes over months.
- **The blocks are not equal:** for a payment service, law weighs more than ecology.

## Scheme

```
Subject · country · horizon · decision
┌──────────────┬──────────────┬──────────────┐
│ P politics   │ E economy    │ S society    │
│ facts, dates │ facts, dates │ facts, dates │
├──────────────┼──────────────┼──────────────┤
│ T technology │ E environment│ L law        │
│ facts, dates │ facts, dates │ facts, dates │
└──────────────┴──────────────┴──────────────┘
Threats │ Opportunities │ Decision, deadline, review trigger
```

## How to apply

1. **Set the frame:** subject, country, horizon, and the decision the analysis will prepare.
2. **Ask the questions for each block.** P: sanctions, taxes, trade policy. E: growth, inflation, rates, exchange rates, incomes. S: demographics, habits, trust. T: new technologies and standards, cyber threats. E: climate and ESG requirements (ESG — environmental, social and governance standards). L: laws, licenses, data protection.
3. **Collect facts:** an event or a rule with a date, a number and a source, not an opinion.
4. **Put each fact in one block only.** A legal norm goes to L, even if a political body adopted it.
5. **Mark each fact:** threat, opportunity or unclear; and through what it acts (demand, costs, permitted actions).
6. **An empty block means "not searched or not found", not "no impact".**
7. **If a priority is needed,** rate threats and opportunities by strength of impact and probability — this is a step beyond the set of facts.
8. **Name the decision:** what we do, what we only monitor, who is responsible, by what deadline, and which event or figure would prompt a revision of the conclusion.

## Example

Tinkoff, a digital bank in Russia. Pavel Kutov, bachelor's thesis (University of Economics and Business in Prague, defended 31.01.2023), analyses the external environment of the Russian banking sector using PESTEL. One or two facts per block:

| Block | Facts from the thesis |
|---|---|
| Politics | Sanctions after 24.02.2022: some banks cut off from Visa, Mastercard and SWIFT; according to S&P Global, assets over 91 of 114.55 trillion rubles were affected (as of 30.09.2021) |
| Economy | S&P lowered credit ratings of Russian banks; investors sold shares |
| Society | Russians withdrew over $1.3 billion from accounts within a few days |
| Technology | Mobile payments, cashback, crypto wallets, ecosystems |
| Environment | Emissions reduction, ESG standards |
| Law | From 1.06.2022 the bank must warn clients about the risks of investment products |

Conclusion: opportunity — Banki.ru expects 25–30 banks to exit in 2022, and their clients may come to Tinkoff; threats — sanctions, weaker partnerships, takeover risk, slower growth.

Decision: recommendations to top management — warn shareholders that the indicators will be weaker; develop the ecosystem by studying Sberbank's services (marketplace, delivery, online pharmacy); check partners during the crisis; avoid drawing Western attention to the new owner.

What the case lacks: priorities of facts, deadlines and owners. The bank did not adopt the student's recommendations, so no adopted decision is shown here. The bank is a fintech, not e-commerce.

Case source: P. Kutov, [«Strategic analysis of Tinkoff bank in the Russian banking market»](https://insis.vse.cz/zp/79917), 2023, sections 2.5 and 3; [thesis record](https://vskp.vse.cz/english/86786).

## Common mistakes

- **Facts without "so what".** A list from which neither threats nor a decision follow.
- **Opinions instead of facts.** "The market is unstable" without a figure or date cannot be checked or revised.
- **Equal weight for all blocks.** Factors differ in strength: blocks filled for form's sake bury the main ones.
- **Replacing the environment with internal matters.** Competitors, prices and the company's own weaknesses do not belong in PESTEL.
- **Double counting.** One law is recorded both under politics and under law.
- **A stale snapshot.** In the case, the panic over withdrawals had subsided by mid-March 2022.
- **Several countries in one analysis.** Rules and habits differ: make a separate analysis for each country.
- **Retellings instead of primary sources.** The case relies heavily on media data; a law or regulator's report is more reliable.
