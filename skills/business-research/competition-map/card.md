# Competition Map

## Essence

A Competition Map shows everyone who competes for a product's customer, step by step along the key scenario. The center of the map is the scenario steps, for example "find a product → buy → receive". Around them are four types of competitors:

- **direct** — solve the same problem in the same way;
- **indirect** — solve the same problem in a different way;
- **displacers** — do not solve this problem, but take the customer's same money or time;
- **channel competition** — compete for access to the customer: where the customer learns about offers and where they start choosing.

The map shows at which step of the scenario you lose the customer, and to whom.

## When to use and when not to

Use it when you enter a market and need to see all competitors, not only similar products; when customers leave and it is unclear where to; when the product covers only part of the scenario and you need to understand who holds the remaining steps.

Do not use it for a detailed feature comparison with one competitor: the map shows who competes and where, but not how much stronger you are.

Limitations:

- **The map does not show competitors' strength.** A large player and a newcomer look the same.
- **The type boundaries are conditional.** The same player can be a direct competitor at one step and a channel at another.
- **The list gets outdated.** New players appear, so the map must be updated.

## Scheme

```
          Direct          │         Indirect
   same problem,         │   same problem,
   same way              │   other way
                  ┌────────┴────────┐
 ─────────────────│ step 1 → step 2 │─────────────────
                  │    → step 3     │
                  └────────┬────────┘
          Channel         │        Displacers
   for customer access    │   for money and time
```

Next to the map, write the conclusion: the main competitor at each step and what to do about it.

## How to apply

1. **Describe the customer's key scenario** in full, including the steps your product does not cover.
2. **For each step, list who else helps the customer** get through it: companies, services, do-it-yourself solutions.
3. **Sort the competitors into the four types.** Check the type with a question: does it solve the same problem, in the same way, and what exactly does it compete for?
4. **Mark the main competitor at each step** — the one the customer goes to most often.
5. **Decide what to do with each main competitor:** outflank it, cover the step yourself, agree on a partnership, or accept losing the step.
6. **Update the map** when new players appear and when the scenario changes.

## Example

In its 2019 annual report (10-K), Amazon listed its competitors in the Competition section. The company did not publish a competition map; below, its list is arranged by type and by the steps of the purchase scenario — this is our arrangement.

| Scenario step | Type | Who Amazon named |
|---|---|---|
| Find a product | channel competition | search engines, price comparison sites, social networks, portals and apps through which buyers search for and buy products |
| Buy | direct | online and omnichannel stores, suppliers, distributors and manufacturers of the same products |
| Buy | indirect | ordinary stores, including grocery stores |
| — | displacers | not named in the section |

Amazon itself named the main competitive factors in retail: assortment, price and convenience, including fast delivery.

Decision: at the search step, Amazon became an advertising platform. In September 2018 the company grouped its advertising products — seller ads in Amazon search, display and video advertising — under one brand, Amazon Advertising. The company itself explained this as simplification rather than competition with search engines; the link to the map is our interpretation.

Result: Amazon's advertising services revenue was $12.6 billion in 2019, $19.8 billion in 2020 and $31.2 billion in 2021, 2.5 times more over two years.

Case sources: Amazon, [10-K for 2019](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872420000004/amzn-20191231x10k.htm), Competition section; Amazon, [10-K for 2021](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000101872422000005/amzn-20211231.htm), revenue by type; AdExchanger, [«Amazon Rebrands Its Ad Products Under Amazon Advertising»](https://www.adexchanger.com/online-advertising/amazon-rebrands-its-ad-products-under-amazon-advertising/) (September 5, 2018).

## Common mistakes

- **Only direct competitors.** A map made of similar products does not show where customers actually go.
- **A scenario made only of your steps.** Steps the product does not cover drop out, and customers are intercepted at those steps.
- **A list without a conclusion.** Competitors are listed, but there is no decision on what to do with the main one at each step.
- **Displacers are not visible.** Competition for the customer's money and time drops out, because these players do not look like you.
