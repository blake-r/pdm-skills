# Gartner's Hype Cycle

## Essence

The Hype Cycle, or technology maturity cycle, is a curve from the analyst firm Gartner: how market expectations of a new technology change over time. The vertical axis shows expectations, the horizontal axis shows time. The curve passes through five phases: a sharp rise of interest, a peak, a trough, a slow climb, and a plateau where the technology delivers value on a mass scale. For each technology, the curve marks how many years it will take to reach the plateau. Gartner publishes such curves every year by industry and topic; they are used to decide when to invest in a technology.

## When to use and when not to

Use it when you decide whether and when to invest in a new technology; when you need to calibrate management's expectations; when you compare several technologies to choose which to invest in now and which to keep watching.

Do not use it to choose a vendor or to calculate the payback of a specific project: the curve describes the market as a whole, not your task.

Limitations:

- **A position on the curve is Gartner's expert assessment** based on market interest and publications, not a measurement of how well the technology works.
- **Not every technology reaches the plateau:** some become obsolete earlier, and Gartner marks this with a separate sign.
- **The market phase is not your readiness.** A trough of interest does not mean the technology will not solve your task; a peak does not mean it will.

## Scheme

| Phase | What happens (per Gartner) |
|---|---|
| **1. Innovation trigger** (called "technology trigger" in early versions) | a breakthrough, first pilots and media interest; ready products are often absent, commercial benefit is not proven |
| **2. Peak of inflated expectations** | publications about successes, often together with many failures; some companies act, most do not |
| **3. Trough of disillusionment** | interest falls: experiments and deployments fail to meet expectations; vendors leave the market; investment continues only if the survivors bring their products to maturity |
| **4. Slope of enlightenment** | it becomes clear where the technology delivers value; second and third generation products; more pilots, cautious companies wait |
| **5. Plateau of productivity** | mass adoption; criteria for choosing a vendor are clear |

The marker on each technology shows the time to the plateau: less than 2 years, 2–5 years, 5–10 years, more than 10 years, or "obsolete before plateau".

## How to apply

1. **Choose a technology** and find the current Gartner curve for your area. If there is no curve, assess the phase yourself using the signs from the scheme and mark it as your own estimate.
2. **Record the phase and the time to the plateau.**
3. **Check it against your task:** which problem the technology solves for you and whether ready products exist for it.
4. **Choose the action:** watch, run a pilot, or adopt. The earlier the phase, the higher the risk and the more important it is to limit investment.
5. **Review the decision** with each new curve: positions change every year.

## Example

ASX, the Australian Securities Exchange, was choosing a replacement for CHESS — a system from the 1990s for recording shares and settling trades. ASX did not use the Gartner curve; below, its decisions are compared with Gartner's positions on blockchain (distributed ledger) in its publications.

| When | Gartner on blockchain | ASX |
|---|---|---|
| August 2017 | blockchain "having passed the peak"; most transformative impact in 5–10 years | tests a distributed ledger system with Digital Asset |
| December 2017 | | decides to replace CHESS with this system; Digital Asset's CEO: after so much hype around distributed ledgers, this is "the first meaningful proof" that the technology can live up to its potential |
| October 2019 | blockchain "sliding into the Trough of Disillusionment"; most enterprise projects are "stuck in experimentation mode" | the project continues |
| November 2022 | | Accenture report: application software about 63% complete, complex integrated solution, vendor management and governance issues |

Decision: in November 2022 ASX paused the project to reassess it and announced a write-off of AUD 245–255 million before tax. A year later it chose a ready product, TCS BaNCS (distributed ledger connectivity possible later), and split the replacement into two stages.

Result: clearing on the new system launched on 20 April 2026, and settlement was estimated in 2023 for 2028–2029. ASX admitted that its 10 February 2022 statement that the project was "progressing well" misled the market; the regulator ASIC proposed a court fine of AUD 20.5 million.

Case sources: ASX — [2017 decision](https://www.asx.com.au/content/dam/asx/about/media-releases/2017/ASX-Selects-DLT-to-Replace-CHESS-Media-Release-7December2017.pdf), [project stop](https://www.asx.com.au/content/dam/asx/about/media-releases/2022/60-17-november-2022-CHESS-Replacement-ASX-reassessing-financial-derecognition_.pdf) (17 November 2022), [TCS selection](https://www.asx.com.au/content/dam/asx/about/media-releases/2023/70-20-november-2023-chess-replacement-solution-announced-and-2024-consultation.pdf) (20 November 2023); [ASIC](https://www.asic.gov.au/about-asic/news-centre/find-a-media-release/2026-releases/26-119mr-asx-admits-misleading-conduct-relating-to-chess-replacement-project) (2026); Gartner — [August 2017](https://web.archive.org/web/20170816152532/https://www.gartner.com/newsroom/id/3784363), [October 2019](https://web.archive.org/web/20191020084405/https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2019-10-08-gartner-2019-hype-cycle-shows-most-blockchain-technologies-are-still-five-to-10-years-away-from-transformational-impact).

## Common mistakes

- **Treating the curve as a project forecast.** The phase describes market expectations, not your team's chances.
- **Entering at the peak out of fear of missing out.** At the peak, stories of success come together with failures.
- **Rejecting what works in the trough.** The technology already solves your task, but it is postponed because the market is disillusioned.
- **Treating the time to plateau as the deployment time.** "5–10 years" is about the market; your project may take less or more.
- **An old curve.** The decision relies on a position from three years ago.
