# Strategy Canvas

## Essence

The Strategy Canvas is a chart on which your offering is compared with competitors' offerings on the factors the industry competes on. The horizontal axis shows the factors: price, service, speed, range. The vertical axis shows the level the buyer gets on each factor. One company's line across all factors is its value curve. If the curves coincide, the companies compete for the same things; the goal is to find your own curve: which factors to eliminate, which to reduce, which to raise, and which to create anew. The canvas was devised by W. Chan Kim and Renée Mauborgne as the main tool of blue ocean strategy.

## When to use and when not to

Use it when a product gets lost among similar offers and competition boils down to price; when you enter a market and look for how to stand out; when you need to show a team or investors how the offer differs from alternatives.

Do not use it for a precise comparison of specifications: the levels on the canvas are an estimate, not a measurement. It does not replace conversations with buyers: the factors of the industry are visible on the canvas, but the value for the buyer is not.

Limitations:

- **Levels are subjective.** Without facts under each rating, the canvas shows the team's opinion.
- **Industry factors are not visible from the inside.** A company tends to list what it is proud of, not what the buyer pays for.
- **The canvas is a snapshot.** Competitors change their offers, and the canvas is redrawn.

## Scheme

```
Level
  5 │ ●───●               ○───○
    │      ╲             ╱
    │       ●───●       ╱
    │            ╲     ╱
  0 │ ○───○───○───●───●
    └────────────────────────────
      F1  F2  F3  F4  F5  F6   factors
      ● competitors  ○ new curve
```

Next to the chart, write what is meant by each factor, and the four actions: eliminate, reduce, raise, create.

## How to apply

1. **Choose the market and the offers to compare:** yours and two or three main alternatives, including what the buyer does without you.
2. **List the factors** the industry competes on and spends money on. Check the list with buyers.
3. **Rate the level** of each offer on each factor on one scale, and write a fact under each rating.
4. **Plot the curves** and find where they coincide.
5. **Draw the new curve:** what to eliminate, reduce, raise and create, so that it differs from the others and leads to lower costs.
6. **Check it with buyers** and redraw the canvas when the market changes.

## Example

Nickel, a French fintech, launched on 11 February 2014 an account with a bank card that is opened at tobacco kiosks. Blue Ocean Strategy lists Nickel as a teaching case (INSEAD); no published canvas was found. Below, the canvas is assembled from public facts; the comparison with banks is qualitative and the levels are our estimate.

| Factor | French banks, 2014 | Nickel |
|---|---|---|
| Service price | annual account and card fees, plus separate paid services | €20 per year account fee (2019 tariff) |
| Overdraft and credit | yes | overdraft not allowed |
| Cheque book | yes | none issued |
| Anyone can open an account | a bank may refuse | no income conditions; about 40% of clients are listed in banking incident registries |
| Opening | appointment and documents at a branch | at a kiosk with ID and a mobile number; the account is usable right away |
| Opening at a tobacco kiosk | no | yes |

Decision: eliminate overdraft, credit and cheques; reduce the price and the client requirements; raise speed; create a channel through tobacco kiosks.

Result: BNP Paribas bought 89.1% of the company on 12 July 2017, when more than 630 thousand accounts had been opened. Nickel was profitable on a monthly basis from August 2017, reached about 1 million accounts in August 2018 and 3 million clients by 2023.

Case sources: Blue Ocean Strategy, [Nickel case description](https://www.blueoceanstrategy.com/teaching-materials/nickel/); MoneyVox, [«Nickel a 5 ans»](https://www.moneyvox.fr/banque-en-ligne/actualites/72415/nickel-a-5-ans-le-compte-des-buralistes-a-t-il-tenu-ses-promesses) (2019); La Finance pour tous, [«Le compte Nickel»](https://www.lafinancepourtous.com/pratique/banque/nouveaux-comptes-et-nouvelles-banques/le-compte-nickel-un-compte-chez-les-buralistes/); BNP Paribas, [deal](https://group.bnpparibas/en/press-release/bnp-paribas-completes-acquisition-compte-nickel) (12 July 2017).

## Common mistakes

- **Factors through the company's eyes.** The list contains internal characteristics, not what the buyer pays for.
- **Estimates without facts.** Levels are assigned by feel.
- **Only raising.** The curve goes up on all factors, and costs go up too; the difference comes from the factors that were eliminated and created.
- **Too many factors.** A canvas with twenty factors cannot be read.
- **Comparing only with direct competitors.** Alternatives the buyer uses right now are overlooked.
