# Value Chain & Nets

## Essence

The value chain breaks a company's activities into types of work and shows which of them make up the value for the buyer and the costs. Michael Porter proposed it in 1985 to find sources of competitive advantage. The model is also applied more broadly: to the chain of companies in an industry (for Porter, the value system) and to the value network, where a company connects customers with each other.

## When to use and when not to

Use it when you need to understand which links make up the price and the lead time for the client, and which link is expensive, slow or outside your control. Input data: the links, their costs and time, the price and time at the output.

Do not use it as a list of departments. Limitations:

- **The chain is designed for a physical product.** Stabell and Fjeldstad: Porter's primary activities concern physical products; for companies that connect clients (a bank, a payment network) the "network" configuration is needed.
- **"Value network" means two different things.** For Stabell and Fjeldstad it is the model of a mediator with three types of activity. For Brandenburger and Nalebuff (value net) it is a diamond of four players: customers, suppliers, competitors and complementors (those who make your product more valuable).
- **Other companies' costs by link are not visible.** From outside, mainly the output price is known: give a figure per link with a source.

## Scheme

```
support: firm infrastructure · HR · technology · procurement
primary: link 1 → link 2 → link 3 → link 4 → link 5   margin ▶
         each: what it does · price · time · what the client sees
For a mediator, instead of links: network promotion and contracts ·
service provision · infrastructure
```

## How to apply

1. **Choose the level** (product, company, industry) **and the final value:** what the client gets at the end, with price and time.
2. **Determine the configuration:** the product is transformed from inputs and passed on — chain; the company connects clients — network.
3. **Break it into links.** Company: five primary activities (inbound logistics, operations, outbound logistics, marketing and sales, service) and four support activities (firm infrastructure, HR, technology, procurement). Industry: participants along the path of the product or money.
4. **Fill in each link:** who, what it does, cost, time, what the client sees. Margin is the value at the end minus the costs of all links.
5. **Find links without value:** cost and time grow, the client sees no benefit, or you do not control the link.
6. **Decide for each link:** improve, take over, replace, remove. Check the neighbors: the costs of other links change.
7. **Write the check:** which client metric (price, time) will change and by what date.

## Example

Wise is a fintech transfer service. In its FY2024 report (financial year to 31 March 2024) it compares a transfer of £200 to Singapore. For a traditional bank the chain is: the sender's bank, an "internal" correspondent bank (an intermediary that holds an account with another bank), an international correspondent bank, a Singapore bank, and the recipient's bank. Along the whole path: rate 1.6604, fee £8.63, markup 3.27% (£6.53), total £15.16; the recipient sees S$306.91 (Singapore dollars) — up to five business days. Wise charges a fee of £4.61 at a rate of 1.7197, delivering S$336.01; 62% of transfers arrive in less than 20 seconds, and 95% within 24 hours.

Decision from the report: «We looked at this problem and decided we would build a new, better network for the world's money» — replace the correspondent chain with direct connections to payment systems. By FY2024: more than 90 local banks and payment providers, 65+ licenses, five direct national payment systems, £118 billion of transfers in the year.

What the case does not include: costs and margins per link (the figures are given for the whole path), and Wise's own chain. The comparison is made by Wise itself: one route, and banks — an average of four in May 2024.

Source of the case: Wise plc, [«Annual Report and Accounts 2024»](https://wise.com/imaginary-v2/images/3f1628373b212ca54c1ac73c68d69b72-WISE-2024-Annual-Report-and-Accounts.pdf), Strategic Report, pp. 14, 15 and 30.

## Common mistakes

- **A chain instead of a network for a mediator:** a bank or marketplace is drawn as "purchase, production, sale".
- **The two meanings of "value network" are mixed.** Brandenburger and Nalebuff's diamond and Stabell and Fjeldstad's network answer different questions.
- **Drawing a process instead of value:** it is not said what the client sees and what they pay for.
- **Invented link costs:** a number without a source is a guess.
- **Forgotten support activities.**
- **Optimizing a link in isolation,** without the links to its neighbors.
- **Taking one author's comparison for the picture of the market:** one route, four banks.
