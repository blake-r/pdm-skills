# Factors for the GE–McKinsey Matrix

A catalog of factor variants for both axes. No source sets a fixed list: the person who uses the matrix picks the factors, weights them and rates them. Take no more than 10 per axis; 5 to 7 is usually enough. Merge overlapping factors. A factor sits on one axis only.

A higher score is always better for the unit: write each factor so that the high end is the favorable one ("few competitors", "low external risk").

## Industry attractiveness (describes the market, the same for every player)

Market:
- Market or segment size
- Market growth rate; long-run growth
- Industry profitability (margins; Porter's Five Forces)
- Number and activity of competitors; competitive intensity
- Barriers to entry
- Industry structure (concentration, bargaining power of buyers and suppliers)
- Capital intensity
- Cyclicality and seasonality
- Price trend

Trends and risks:
- Potential for market development
- Product life cycle stage of the industry
- Changes in demand
- Potential external risks (macro environment; PESTEL)
- Risk and volatility

Customers:
- Audience of the market and consumption culture
- Customers' loyalty to existing competitors
- Unmet needs
- Market segmentation

Technology and regulation:
- Pace of technological change; disruption risk
- Regulatory barriers and requirements
- Availability of the resources the business needs, including labor

## Competitive strength (describes the unit against its rivals)

- Relative market share and its trend (share growth against rivals)
- Brand recognition, brand strength and customer loyalty
- Uniqueness of the offer; level of product differentiation
- Product quality
- Cost position; operational efficiency
- Profitability of the unit against rivals
- Financial resources
- Marketing capabilities; sales force performance
- Distribution reach; customer access and retention
- Technical capability; intellectual property and patents
- Expertise and professionalism of the staff; depth of management
- Speed of reaction to market changes
- Strength in the industry's critical success factors
- Value chain strength; supply chain resilience
- Production flexibility
- Data or network effects
- Fit with the rest of the company (synergy), when the matrix is used inside a portfolio

## Form of a filled table

One table per axis: criterion, weight (percent, summing to 100), rating (1 to 5 or 0 to 10), result (weight times rating); the last row is the sum. Rows are ordered by weight. Example of a row: "Financial resources, 16%, rating 10, result 1.60".

A factor sits on one axis only. For example, the company's own market share describes the unit, so it goes to strength and not to attractiveness.

## Where the variants come from

Public guides agree that the lists are open-ended: [Strategic Management Insight](https://strategicmanagementinsight.com/tools/ge-mckinsey-matrix/) (11 attractiveness and 10 strength factors, "no definite list"), [Think Insights](https://thinkinsights.net/strategy/ge-mckinsey-nine-box-matrix), [Umbrex](https://umbrex.com/resources/frameworks/strategy-frameworks/ge-mckinsey-nine-box-matrix/) (6 to 12 factors per axis, "more is not better"), [Marketing Teacher](https://www.marketingteacher.com/the-general-electric-business-screen/), [Pipedrive](https://www.pipedrive.com/en/blog/ge-mckinsey-matrix) (five questions per axis). These are secondary guides, not the original GE material. The lists also include variants used by practitioners in training and consulting.
