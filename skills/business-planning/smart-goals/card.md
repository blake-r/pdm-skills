# SMART Goals

## Essence

SMART is a checklist of five questions for wording a goal. It helps write a goal so that it has an executor and so that one can say whether it has been achieved. In George Doran's original (1981) the letters stand for: Specific, Measurable, Assignable (someone is named to do it), Realistic, Time-related.

## When to use and when not to

Use it when a team's or a manager's goal must be written down for agreement with the executor and management. Inputs: a rough goal, the current value of the indicator, resources, the executor, the deadline.

Do not use it to choose goals: it checks the wording, not whether the goal is worth pursuing.

Limits:

- **Not all five criteria are mandatory.** Doran: not every goal will get all five; the closer the wording is to them, the "smarter" it is.
- **A number is not possible everywhere.** Doran: a goal cannot always be expressed quantitatively at every level, especially for middle managers in staff roles.
- **Labels do not matter.** Doran (p. 35): the dispute over the difference between goals and objectives is a waste of time; the distinction is useful only at the management level: goals are long-term principles, objectives give them a quantitative expression. The card's conclusion: SMART is applied to objectives.

## Scheme

```
S  Specific       — which area of improvement
M  Measurable     — a number or progress indicator: from what → to what
A  Assignable     — who exactly does it
R  Realistic      — achievable with the available resources
T  Time-related   — by which date
+  Action plan    — steps toward the goal
```

## How to apply

1. **Write a draft goal** and find the current value of the indicator: without it you can neither measure the shift nor assess realism.
2. **S.** Name one area of improvement; if there are two, split the goal.
3. **M.** State a number and a unit: "from X to Y". If there is no number, name a progress indicator.
4. **A.** Enter one executor.
5. **R.** Compare the goal with the resources (people, money, time) and write the ceiling into the goal itself, as in the example below.
6. **T.** State a date; for a long goal add interim checkpoints.
7. **Add an action plan.** Doran: the pair "goal plus plan" matters, not the goal alone.
8. **Decide.** If all five answers are present, the goal is accepted. If A, R or T is missing, the goal goes back to its author (the card's rule: Doran does not require all five criteria). If there is no number in M, agree on an indicator or record that it cannot be measured.

## Example

No public e-commerce or fintech case with a SMART goal and an accepted decision was found: there are only teaching examples. So here is an example from Doran's article:

"To develop and implement by December 31, 198_ an inventory system that will reduce inventory costs by $1 million, with a cost not to exceed 200 work hours and $15,000 out-of-pocket initial expenditures."

| Criterion | What the wording says |
|---|---|
| S | inventory costs; means — an inventory system |
| M | reduction of costs by $1 million |
| A | executor not named |
| R | resource ceiling: 200 work hours and $15,000 |
| T | by December 31, 198_ (year not given) |

What is missing: e-commerce and fintech, the company, the executor, the plan, the result and the decision. Doran gives the example before the acronym as a model of an objective; the breakdown by S/M/A/R/T and the assignment of the ceiling to R are made by the card.

Example source: G. T. Doran, [1981 article](https://www.decisionskills.com/uploads/5/1/6/0/5160560/doran_1981_s.m.a.r.t-way-management-review.pdf), p. 35.

## Common mistakes

Items without a reference to Doran are the card's conclusions.

- **Mixing up the decoding versions.** Later retellings read A as Achievable, R as Relevant, T as Time-bound, for example [an article on Habr](https://habr.com/ru/companies/rshb/articles/802067/), 2024. The card's observation: Achievable is close to Doran's Realistic, Relevant is not equal to it, and Assignable drops out of the criteria, although in the article's algorithm assigning an executor remains a step. Agree on a version.
- **A goal without an executor:** it belongs to everyone and to no one.
- **Measurability at any price.** Doran: one can lose the benefit of a more abstract goal for the sake of a quantitative one.
- **A goal without an action plan.** Doran: the pair "goal and plan" matters.
- **A number without a starting level.** "Reduce by $1 million" cannot be checked for realism while current costs are unknown.
- **A means instead of a result.** "Implement a system" is not a goal: in the example the result is the cost reduction.
- **Five "yes" answers taken as proof that the goal is right.** The check does not show that the goal is worth the resources.
