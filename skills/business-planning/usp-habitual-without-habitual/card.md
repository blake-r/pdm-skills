# USP "Familiar without familiar"

## Essence

A USP (unique selling proposition) is a short phrase about what sets an offer apart. In this form it is built as "familiar without familiar": you name something familiar to the customer and remove from it what the customer considers indispensable. Examples from PAF (Product Architecture Framework, productframework.ru): "A bank without branches", "A taxi without phone dispatchers".

## When to use and when not to

Use it when the product belongs to a familiar category and the main difference is the refusal of a familiar element that is a burden to the customer (a queue, a visit, a call, waiting). Inputs: the category, the removed element and the reason the customer dislikes it.

Do not use it if the difference lies in something added, or if the customer needs the removed element.

Limits:

- **A refusal is not a benefit.** "Without branches" says what is absent, but not what the customer gets: explain the replacement.
- **The formula does not prove demand:** that this particular element bothered customers is confirmed by conversations or data.

## Scheme

```
[Familiar] without [familiar]
  category      what the customer considers obligatory and you removed
```

The template file adds "replacement", "reason", "phrase", "risk", "check" and "decision" to the two parts; PAF does not have them.

## How to apply

1. **Name the category** the way the customer names it: bank, taxi, phone.
2. **List the familiar elements** and mark those customers complain about.
3. **Choose one element** for "without" and check that you removed it rather than hid it.
4. **Define the replacement:** how the customer gets what the removed element used to provide.
5. **Assemble the phrase** "category without element" and read it through the customer's eyes: is it clear without explanation?
6. **Name the risk:** which tasks the removed element solved (a branch accepted documents and signatures) and how you cover them now.
7. **Decide:** whether you accept the wording and how you will check that customers value the refusal.

## Example

Tinkoff Credit Systems (TCS, later Tinkoff, now T-Bank), Russia, fintech. The bank was founded in 2006 by Oleg Tinkov. The form "a bank without branches" is visible in its self-description: in an interview with Retail Banker International, Oliver Hughes, president of TCS, says "We're very much a virtual operator", and about taking deposits — "as a virtual, branchless bank". There is no slogan in the text: the phrase in the form of the formula is our breakdown.

| Part | What the source says |
|---|---|
| Familiar | bank (full license; Hughes: "we are not a bank"), credit cards and payments |
| Without the familiar | without branches |
| Replaced by | couriers (1000 in 600 cities per the text; "almost 1000 couriers and agents, almost 600 cities" per the sidebar); direct mail as the main acquisition channel in 2007 |
| Why | wide reach without the costs of maintaining branches: "We have a long reach. Our customer channels gives us access to the entire market with none of the upkeep cost" |
| Risk | under Russian rules, accepting a deposit requires an in-person meeting and a signature; people do not want to hand money to a bank they cannot see |

The choice: deposits are accepted only in person; the company chose couriers and online acquisition instead of branches. Hughes: the company tested several models, and for deposits online acquisition with couriers worked best. The result per the sidebar: more than $977 million in deposits in less than four years after the launch of the online deposit program.

Not in the case: the advertising phrase itself, customer testing, costs, the decision date, the names of the tested models. This is not a published USP.

Source: B. Bambrough, ["Tinkoff steps up to the Russian leaders"](https://www.retailbankerinternational.com/features/tinkoff-steps-up-to-the-russian-leaders/), 23.10.2013.

## Common mistakes

- **Removing what the customer needs.** Some people need an in-person meeting; check who is left without the service.
- **The phrase says only what is absent,** not what the customer gets.
- **The element is hidden, not removed:** "no calls" when the bank calls is a new objection.
- **The refusal runs into rules,** and the promise cannot be kept.
- **A difference taken for an advantage:** whether customers value the refusal is unknown without talking to them.
- **No check after launch.** Nobody counts how customers cover the tasks of the removed element.
