# USP 4U Formula

## Essence

A USP (unique selling proposition) is a short phrase in which the customer sees what they will get and why they should choose exactly you. The 4U formula checks such a phrase against four features whose English names begin with U: useful, unique, ultra-specific (that is, maximum concreteness) and urgent. It is used as a checklist for a headline or an ad line.

## When to use and when not to

Use it when there is a draft headline or ad and you need to understand what it lacks. Inputs: the customer's benefit, a verifiable number, the reason the benefit is achievable, a deadline if there is one.

Do not use it as proof of a difference from competitors.

Limits:

- **The number must be verifiable.** Specificity without data is invention; "up to 30%" requires calculating the conditions.
- **Urgency is not mandatory.** PAF explicitly says it may be absent. A deadline that is regularly extended undermines trust.
- **The word "unique" is understood differently.** In PAF it is an explanation of how the benefit is achieved, ideally with differentiation from competitors; PAF focuses on the mechanism, while AWAI and Widenhouse focus on difference and surprise. Agree on which meaning you use.

## Scheme

| Feature | Question to the phrase | Yes / no |
|---|---|---|
| Useful | What customer problem or benefit does the phrase name? | |
| Unique | What makes the benefit achievable, how is it different? | |
| Ultra-specific | Is there a measurable number (percent, rubles, pieces, minutes)? | |
| Urgent | Is there a deadline or a reason to act now? | |

## How to apply

1. **Write down** the draft phrase and the audience.
2. **Write the customer's benefit** in one sentence, in their words.
3. **Find the mechanism:** what in the product or model produces this benefit. This is the second part of the phrase.
4. **Add a number** and check it against data: conditions, sample, average result or maximum ("on average" and "up to" are different promises).
5. **Decide about the deadline:** is there a real limit (trial period, price change date)? If not, leave the feature empty.
6. **Assemble the phrase** and read it through the customer's eyes. An empty feature is a decision: add it or omit it.
7. **Record the decision:** publish, rewrite, or first collect data for the number.

## Example

Wise (formerly TransferWise, international money transfers, fintech), a company blog post dated 21.09.2020: "Closer to Mission 0: transfers are now cheaper for 76% of our customers". The 4U breakdown is ours; the company does not mention the formula.

| Feature | What the source says |
|---|---|
| Useful | transfers are cheaper |
| Unique (in PAF's sense: how it is achieved) | the company names the reasons: more customers — higher efficiency; faster transfers — lower operating costs |
| Ultra-specific | 76% of customers; on average 15% cheaper, in some cases up to 30%; a £1000 transfer to IDR: fee £5.21 → £3.62 |
| Urgent | no term or deadline; there is a change date, 21 September 2020 |

Decision: from 21.09.2020 Wise cut transfer prices from USD or GBP to any country, and from any country to a number of currencies (INR, THB, IDR and others). The motive is named: getting closer to "Mission 0" (the goal of eventually making international transfers free). The same post contains Wise's own claim: "already 8 times cheaper than a bank".

Not in the case: this is an announcement of a price cut, not a check of an ad phrase; there is no methodology for the "8x" claim and no results after the cut; there is no urgency.

Source: [Wise Blog, Abhishek Choudhari, 21.09.2020](https://wise.com/gb/blog/transfers-are-now-cheaper-for-76-of-our-customers).

## Common mistakes

- **A number without grounds.** "Up to 30%" without conditions is read as ordinary.
- **A benefit without a mechanism.** The promise sounds like everyone else's.
- **Invented urgency.** A permanent "last week of the promotion" teaches the customer not to believe deadlines.
- **All four features at any cost.** The phrase does not read well; remove the weak feature.
- **Mixing up the meanings of "unique".** One means the mechanism, the other means being different from others; the phrase is checked against different criteria.
- **No check after publication.** It is unclear what drove the response.

## Source

Description in PAF (Sergey Tikhomirov, productframework.ru, CC BY-SA 4.0, undated): "useful + unique + ultra-specific + urgent; urgency may be absent"; the author of the method is not named in PAF.

In English-language sources the formula is attributed to copywriter Michael Masterson: an AWAI (American Writers & Artists Institute) page dated 18.06.2001 names Masterson and Bill Bonner as the authors of four techniques for strengthening bullets (short statements of advantages) ("The Secret of the 4 U's"; the page title is "Bullet Blaster: A Review of the 4 U's Plus Exercises…", the author of the page is not named) and defines them as [urgency, uniqueness, ultra-specificity, usefulness](https://www.awai.com/2001/06/a-review-of-the-4-us/). Bob Bly, a contemporary copywriter, writes in his [e-mail marketing report](https://www.bly.com/Report.pdf) that the 4 U's formula was "originally developed by my colleague Michael Masterson". For headlines, the formula is attributed to Masterson in an article by Kathy Widenhouse dated 21.04.2017: [Copywriter Collective](https://copywritercollective.com/the-4-us-use-this-checklist-for-writing-powerful-headlines/).
